How much farther is school?

PPS has proposed closing 14 schools (Scenario A) or 11 (Scenario B). This page estimates how each closure changes the trip to school for the families who live in its attendance area, walking, biking, and by car or bus. It covers K–5 closures and the middle-school moves that come with them.

How this was built

Rebuilding PPS's proposed boundaries

PPS's Oct 6, 2026 board packet includes the superintendent's memo and 36×48-inch attendance-area maps for Scenario A, Scenario B and the status quo at each grade band. The maps are vector PDFs exported from ArcGIS Pro, with no geographic coordinates.

  1. Georeferencing. The boundary lines were extracted from the status-quo K–5 map and fitted to the City of Portland's current attendance-area layer, first with school labels as control points and then by iterative closest-point matching on the lines themselves. Median distance between the map's lines and the city layer after fitting: 0.6 ft. All the scenario maps share the same frame, so the same transform applies to A and B.
  2. Erased and new lines. A boundary counts as erased when it is drawn on the status-quo map but not on the scenario map, and it borders a closing school. New lines inside a closing school's area split that area into pieces.
  3. Receivers. The memo names every receiving school, at most two per closure. Each piece of a closing school's area goes to one of those receivers: the only one, or, for a two-way split, whichever receiver is the shorter average walk for that piece.
  4. Checks against the memo. The memo gives split percentages for three closures. The rebuilt splits, weighted by children living in each piece: Maplewood 66 / 34 to Hayhurst / Rieke (memo: 70 / 30); Stephenson 72 / 28 to Markham / Capitol Hill (memo: 70 / 30); Buckman 56 / 44 to Abernethy / Sunnyside (memo: 50 / 50).
  5. Other shifts. Two boundary moves between schools that stay open are described in writing and included: Duniway's southern edge to Llewellyn (A) and part of Whitman to Lewis (B). Both scenarios list more than 30 other "boundary change" schools. Those smaller adjustments could not be read reliably from the maps and are not modeled.
  6. Special cases. Lent and Rigler enroll only immersion students. Neighborhood students from the Lent area attend Marysville today, and the slides send them to Kelly. Those from the Rigler area attend Scott. César Chávez becomes a Spanish immersion hub, so its neighborhood K–5 students go to Rosa Parks.
Measuring travel time

Travel times come from Felt's isochrone() SQL function, which runs on a self-hosted Valhalla routing engine using OpenStreetMap streets. For each of the 67 elementary, K–8 and middle schools with an attendance area, nested isochrones were traced at fine steps: walking every 3 minutes to an hour, then up to 105 minutes; biking every 2 minutes to 30, then up to 50; driving every minute to 15, then 17, 20 and 25. That is about 4,100 isochrones.

The district was divided into about 3,900 hexagons, each about 0.1 km² (H3 resolution 9). For each hexagon and school, the time is read from the smallest isochrone containing the hexagon's center and interpolated by distance between neighboring bands.

  • Walking uses Valhalla's default adult pace, about 5.1 km/h. Young children walk slower, so real walking times are likely longer.
  • Biking uses Valhalla's default bicycle profile, which favors bike-friendly streets.
  • Driving is free-flow time with no traffic, parking or drop-off line. It also stands in for the school bus here. Real bus rides are longer because routes make stops.
  • Cross-check. ppsdata.info computed driving times between 119 pairs of schools with a different routing engine (OSRM). The two sets agree closely (correlation 0.96). Valhalla's times run about 19% shorter, a typical gap between free-flow engines.
  • One mile of walking is about 19 minutes at this pace. PPS buses K–5 students who live more than 1 mile from school and middle schoolers beyond 1.5 miles (about 28 minutes). The "over 1 mile" figures use those minute thresholds as a proxy for bus eligibility.
Who counts as a student here

Where school-age children live comes from the 2020 Census Demographic and Housing Characteristics file, table P12, at the census-block level: ages 5–9 plus a fifth of ages 10–14 for K–5, and three fifths of ages 10–14 for grades 6–8. Block counts are split into the hexagons by area. Each school's 2025–26 K–5 enrollment (or grades 6–8 for middle schools), minus its immersion enrollment, is spread over its area in proportion to those children. With "Weight by number of students" on, each hexagon's opacity follows that estimate, so long trips where few students live fade out. Block-level Census counts include deliberate statistical noise for privacy, which mostly averages out at the hexagon scale. That estimates where each school's neighborhood students live. Immersion students are not in the travel averages, because their homes can be anywhere in the city. The program moves (Spanish to César Chávez, Mandarin to Woodstock and Boise-Eliot/Humboldt, Vietnamese to Vestal in A) will lengthen many of their trips too.

Building loads in the trade-off chart add each school's immersion students, following the program moves, to its neighborhood students. Capacity is the 2021 Long-Range Facility Plan's functional capacity. These are 2025–26 estimates; PPS's own figures use 2031–32 projections and will differ.

The other boundary approaches

All five approaches close the same schools and make the same program and grade-level changes. They differ in where students from closing schools are sent: K–5 students from closing elementary schools, and grade 6–8 students from Sellwood and the seven K–8 schools that become K–5. The same rule is applied to both, using each middle school's functional capacity. The alternatives leave the two optional middle-school area shifts in PPS's plan (Maplewood to Gray, Irvington to Beaumont) where they are.

ApproachRule
PPS planPPS's receivers and lines, rebuilt from its maps as described above.
PPS rules, shortest tripsKeeps PPS's own rules: at most two receivers per closure, near the building's capacity. Among the six nearest open schools, it picks the one or two that minimize total walking time. Each home goes to the closer of the pair.
Shortest walks within capacityA linear program that sends every home to an open school, minimizing total walking time while keeping each building under 95% of functional capacity. There is no limit on how many schools a closure's area splits among.
Nearest open schoolEvery home goes to the closest open neighborhood school by walking time, ignoring capacity. This is the floor on how little a closure can add.
Whole school to one receiverEach closing school's whole area goes to the single open school with the shortest average walk, which keeps classmates together.
Paths considered and set aside
  • Polygonizing the PDF lines directly. Gaps where lines meet the river or the map frame let neighboring areas bleed together. The approach was replaced with "current GIS polygons, minus erased lines, plus new lines."
  • Labeling map areas by their text labels. Labels sit at uneven offsets from school symbols and sometimes merge with street names. School building locations were used instead.
  • Reading every boundary shift between open schools from the maps. River and frame edges look like erased lines on one map and not the other. Only the two shifts PPS describes in writing were kept.
  • The City's older open-data boundary layer. It predates the 2025 Southeast changes (no Kellogg or Harriet Tubman). The current City of Portland School Boundaries service, last edited Nov 3, 2025, was used instead.
  • Census API for age detail. The API now requires a key, so the bulk 2020 files were used instead: the redistricting file for children under 18, and the DHC file for ages 5–9 and 10–14. Newer American Community Survey estimates exist only for larger block groups and carry sampling error, so the 2020 block counts were kept.
  • Live Felt map embeds driven by the JS SDK. This page runs in a sandbox that blocks third-party frames, so the maps are drawn here from the same Felt-computed data over a Felt-rendered basemap. The Felt maps are linked below.
Limitations
  • The rebuilt K–5 lines follow PPS's maps closely where closures are involved, but are drawn on a ~0.1 km² hexagon grid. Homes within a block or two of a new line may be assigned to the wrong side.
  • About 30 smaller boundary changes at open schools are not modeled, and the middle-school lines follow the memo's rules rather than the 6–8 maps.
  • About 7 in 10 PPS students attend their neighborhood school. Families using transfers, focus options or charters are not modeled separately.
  • Walk times assume an adult pace on any public street or path, and ignore hills, busy crossings and hazard-based busing.
  • Driving and bus times are free-flow. Bus routes, stops and bell times are not modeled.
